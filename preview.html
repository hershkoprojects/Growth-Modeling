The core question

A forecast tells you where you want to go. A growth model shows what needs to happen to get there.

Your target: $5M ARR

To know whether that target is plausible, the model asks:

  • How many paying customers?
  • At what ARPU or ACV?
  • At what conversion rate?
  • From how much traffic or pipeline?
  • With what retention?
  • At what acquisition cost?

The goal isn't to predict the future perfectly. It's to make the assumptions behind it explicit.

Growth Model ≠ Financial Model

A growth model is not a financial model.

Financial model

What will revenue, costs and cash flow look like?

Growth model

What actually has to happen for that revenue to exist?

Instead of starting from accounting lines, it starts from the drivers: acquisition, conversion, pricing, usage, retention, referral, sales.

Building your first growth model

Most people think a growth model is a spreadsheet. It isn't. A spreadsheet is where the model lives — but the model itself is a set of relationships: how one number turns into the next.

Before you type a single formula, map the drivers that connect a visitor to revenue, then put a real rate on every step. Once every edge has a number, the picture starts doing arithmetic for you.

That's the point of the model: to find the lever with the biggest downstream effect before you spend a shekel on it.

About

Why Growth Modeling?

Founders routinely create ambitious growth and revenue projections. But the operating logic behind those projections — what actually has to happen for the numbers to work — usually stays buried inside assumptions.

Growth Modeling exists to bring that logic to the surface: to make it visible, quantitative, testable, explainable, and updatable. The focus sits specifically at the intersection of growth, strategy, modeling, and startup decision-making.

The goal isn't to predict the future perfectly. It's to help founders understand what needs to be true for their startup to grow — and to keep that understanding alive after launch.
